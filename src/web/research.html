Installed generation capacity by technology

Installed net capacity is the maximum generating capability recorded by the transmission-system data provider for units of at least 1 MW, grouped by production technology. It describes the fleet available on 1 January of each year—not the electricity actually produced.

Method. Annual MW values are displayed as published by ENTSO-E (document type A68). Technologies are not interpolated between reporting years. A missing technology/year remains missing.

Electricity generation by technology

This graph shows the electrical energy produced each month by each technology. It answers “what generated the electricity?”, whereas the capacity graph answers “what generating equipment was installed?”.

Loading official generation data…

Method. Each published average MW observation is multiplied by its native interval duration and summed to monthly GWh (document type A75). Hourly and 15-minute observations therefore receive their correct duration weights. Missing source intervals are not replaced with zero; months below 99% observed-hour coverage are flagged above the graph.

When and why do electricity prices turn negative in Spain?

Negative prices are a recent feature of the available Spanish day-ahead history. In this snapshot, they first appear in 2024 and are concentrated in daylight delivery periods. The data shows when the outcome occurs; it does not, by itself, establish that renewable output or demand caused it.

Method and limits

The snapshot uses Spanish OMIE day-ahead observations from 1 January 2018 through 20 August 2026. A negative observation is one with a price below 0 €/MWh. Hourly and 15-minute native observations are counted as published; no interpolation or gap filling is applied. Solar, wind, demand, imports and exports are not currently joined to this research note, so causal explanations remain hypotheses for a later data phase.
